# Design Guides Adapted for Rogue Command

This reference adapts public modeling, shading, bake, and RTS art-direction material to Rogue Command. It sets an art workflow; the pinned Blender 4.5.14, Unity 2021.3.45f2, shader, importer, mesh, and AssetBundle contracts remain authoritative.

## Findings and project decisions

| Source finding | Rogue Command decision |
|---|---|
| Blender Studio's hard-surface workflow begins from measured references and simple blockout geometry, then tests movement, refines shapes, and checks the model from several views. Boolean-heavy refinements can need topology cleanup to restore smooth curvature. | Use generators and primitives to establish scale and function. Refine the primary hull, torso, fairing, or facility shell before adding small parts. Review intersections and moving clearances; repair pinching or stepped curvature after cuts and before UV/atlas work. |
| Blizzard's StarCraft Remastered team kept familiar unit silhouettes so players could recognize units while seeing more detail. | Keep role cues, not every inherited body-plan convention. Compare against the closest native/mod counterpart; if the draft still reads as a stock pattern, sketch two structurally distinct blockouts from the unit's mobility and systems. Add original stance, proportions, negative space, and construction language while preserving fast role recognition. This divergence pass is a Rogue Command design exercise, not a claimed Blizzard production rule. |
| Blender's Bevel and normal tools alter edge geometry and shading; normal maps can transfer high-detail surface information to a UV-mapped mesh, with cages, projection distances, and island margins controlling bake artifacts. | Shape large masses in geometry. Apply bevels selectively, preserve plate facets, and use the existing Normal atlas for fine relief after the shape and UVs stabilize. Inspect seams and padding at target resolution. |
| Blender's Weighted Normal modifier adjusts custom vertex-corner normals using face area, corner angle, and optional face strength. | Use it only to preserve the intended shading of an already sound shape. Do not use weighted normals or smooth shading to hide poor cross-sections, lumpy topology, or an unclear silhouette. |
| Blender's bevel reference notes that real edges are rarely perfectly sharp and that bevels catch light, while bevel width, segments, and limits control where the treatment applies. | Use bevels to describe the component's edge and fabrication scale. Keep critical curves and section changes in the authored form; do not apply a uniform bevel to every part. |
| Unity 2021.3 imports authored mesh normals and tangents, and uses screen-relative object size to switch LODs. | Preserve the project's authored normal/tangent path. Review each LOD at its actual screen size and keep role-defining silhouette and mechanisms readable. The project caps LOD0 at 10,000 Blender source faces; this is not a Unity limit. |
| Blizzard's RTS art guidance prioritizes unit readability, limits distracting palette competition, and notes that team-colored units can disappear against similar terrain colors. | Preview the model's intended color grouping against representative light, dark, and similar-hue backgrounds. Treat this as a local art check; do not imply that game-side faction tinting is implemented unless separately verified. |
| The project shader encodes material and wear data in the four named maps plus the mesh Color RGBA attribute. | Keep the current shader and importer contract. Do not add AO maps or repack shader-unconsumed channels without a separate verified shader change. |
| Unity 2021.3's metallic workflow allows different surface regions in one texture to use different metallic and smoothness response; the Standard Shader reads metallic from R and smoothness from A. | Distinguish painted coating from exposed metal, and separate rubber, glass, and emissive hardware by response. Follow the mod shader's verified R/A reads and pack `1 - roughness` into smoothness A; do not flatten all surfaces to one metallic/smoothness profile. |
| PBR material references treat metalness and roughness as distinct controls for reflected light; roughness variation represents surface-scale variation, not arbitrary color noise. | Keep finish variation tied to material and use. Use sparse, purposeful emission for actual lights or energy paths, and reject procedural edge glow or microtexture that swamps the asset at RTS scale. The cited OpenPBR guide is a material concept reference, not a replacement shader specification. |

These choices are project adaptations, not rules claimed by the cited studios or software vendors.

## Authoring sequence

1. **Write a visual brief.** Record tactical role, mobility, primary weapon/system, scale, and two or three silhouette cues. Collect front, side, and top references for proportions, machinery, and materials. Use reference to understand construction; combine and alter it into an original design.
2. **Run a roster-divergence pass.** Compare the nearest existing unit and note its standard body plan, recognizable role cues, and any repeated game-wide shape convention. If the new blockout still follows that pattern, draft two structurally different silhouettes based on its movement, weapon, cooling/energy, or production needs. Select one and state the design thesis as role constraint → shape choice → functional evidence. Keep the role cues; change the body-plan language. This is a local workflow heuristic, not an industry-wide requirement.
3. **Block out primary masses and movement.** Match the native gameplay footprint, ground contact, weapon clearance, pivots, rig, and required sockets. Test a rough articulation or motion envelope now, while collisions and support changes are still cheap. Use the generated geometry as a blockout whenever the major surfaces still look like unmodified boxes or cylinders.
4. **Pass the silhouette gate.** Render black silhouettes from the elevated game camera and from front/side/top. Compare with the nearest counterpart and nearby archetypes. Check that the unit reads at actual gameplay scale and shows an intentional body-plan departure without losing its role cues. Exaggerate only the features that disappear at that scale.
5. **Shape the main body.** Replace primitive stacks that define visible primary masses with tapered profiles, lofted sections, superellipse shells, planned plate transitions, or sculpted forms suited to the archetype. Keep broad planar plates where they communicate armor or fabrication; use curved surfaces where they communicate cast, formed, aerodynamic, or soft structures.
6. **Resolve mechanical logic.** For each prominent mechanism, check its support, load path, energy or fluid path, clearance, articulation, and connection to adjacent parts. Apply only role-relevant cues: a bike does not need tank armor; a walker needs clear joints and supported feet; a factory needs a comprehensible process or logistics path.
7. **Refine edge and shading transitions.** Inspect a neutral clay render under grazing light. Use flat shading for deliberate facets, smooth shading for curved forms, and bevel widths/segments that describe the edge. Weighted normals can help keep broad faces stable but cannot repair a weak body form.
8. **Allocate detail by scale and surface role.** Geometry carries the outline, cross-section changes, mechanical gaps, and parts that visibly articulate. Existing texture maps carry color, material identity, markings, and only the microrelief that survives at gameplay scale. Any high-to-low bake technique must remain inside the mandatory Material Maker workflow and current four-map contract; Blender baking is not a replacement final-map pipeline. Keep dirt and wear localized to plausible contact, exposed-edge, exhaust, or service areas instead of laying uniform noise over the asset. Give painted coating a non-metal response and distinguish exposed metal, rubber, glass, and emissive components by their physical finish. Preserve MetallicGloss.R as metallic, MetallicGloss.A as smoothness (1 - roughness), linear import with alpha from input, and the mesh Color RGBA vertex-wear data unchanged.
9. **Review camera, color, budget, and LOD.** Compare clay, silhouette, and textured renders at actual screen sizes. Preview the intended color grouping against contrasting and similar-hue backgrounds so the unit does not blend into terrain. If LODs are authored, inspect their screen-size transitions and retain the role-defining outline and equipment. Keep LOD0 at or below 10,000 source faces without treating the value as a target to fill. Track exported Unity triangles separately. Remove duplicate and hidden geometry, then simplify redundant bevels and sub-pixel features while preserving visible armor, silhouette, function, UVs, and authored shading; redesign intersecting parts instead of deleting them just to meet the cap.
10. **Verify the delivered source.** Use the pinned Blender and Unity versions. Inspect effective importer settings, UV coverage, map channels, vertex colors, LODs, sockets, hierarchy, transforms, ground contact, and rig bindings after rebuild. Label results as static inspection, local Unity validation, or in-game runtime evidence.

## Review checklist

- Does the unit read from silhouette alone at the elevated gameplay camera?
- Does a side-by-side comparison show a distinct body plan from the nearest counterpart while retaining fast role recognition?
- Does its intended color grouping remain distinguishable against terrain values that resemble its main hue?
- Are its large forms original and coherent, rather than a stack of unrelated primitives?
- Do visible parts connect, move, and clear one another in a way that fits the archetype?
- After boolean or panel-cut operations, are there any pinched highlights, stepped curvature, or lumpy transitions that need topology repair?
- Do material, curvature, and edge transitions communicate how the part is made?
- Did the exact candidate maps pass a visual check on the target UV model at RTS scale, separately from file/channel validation?
- Does each secondary detail support function, scale, or unit identity at RTS scale?
- Are UVs and tangent-space bake padding adequate for the final atlas and mipmaps?
- Does the mesh remain within project bounds, preserve required sockets/hierarchy/Color RGBA, and stay at or below the strict 10,000-face LOD0 maximum without removing visible or functional parts to force the count?
- Do armor plates, mounts, wheels, barrels, and housings meet at deliberate joints, with no exposed surfaces crossing through adjacent geometry?
- Were Blender faces, Unity triangles, and runtime appearance reported as separate evidence?

- [Blender Studio: Artist Guide Modeling](https://studio.blender.org/tools/artist-guide/asset-creation/modeling) — measured references, functional blockout, and a simplified animated version for design checks.

- [Blender 4.5: Cycles baking](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.5/render/cycles/baking.html) — tangent-space normal bakes, selected-to-active projection, cages, and UV-island margins.
- [Unity 2021.3: Metallic workflow](https://docs.unity3d.com/2021.3/Documentation/Manual/StandardShaderMaterialParameterMetallic.html) and [Smoothness](https://docs.unity3d.com/2021.3/Documentation/Manual/StandardShaderMaterialParameterSmoothness.html) — surface-dependent metallic response and smoothness-map behavior.
- [Blizzard: Behind the Scenes of StarCraft: Remastered](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/20726732/behind-the-scenes-of-starcraft-remastered) — retaining recognizable unit silhouettes while updating visual detail.
- [Blizzard: Mapmaking Best Practices in Art and Performance](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/20097658/mapmaking-best-practices-in-art-and-performance) — prioritizing unit readability and avoiding palette competition with team colors.
- [Unity 2021.3: Model Importer](https://docs.unity3d.com/2021.3/Documentation/Manual/FBXImporter-Model.html) and [Level of Detail](https://docs.unity3d.com/2021.3/Documentation/Manual/LevelOfDetail.html) — authored normals/tangents and distance-based LOD.
- [Adobe Substance 3D: OpenPBR overview](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/substance-3d/general-knowledge/openpbr/openpbr-overview) — material-response concepts for metalness and microscopic roughness; not a Rogue Command shader contract.
- [Epic: Talisman environment template](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/talisman-environment-template-in-unreal-editor-for-fortnite) — a cross-engine example of mid-poly hard-surface assets and deliberate normal treatment; use only as technique reference, not as a Rogue Command pipeline requirement.
